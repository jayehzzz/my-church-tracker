## Authoritative records and supported writes

- `attendance` and present `meeting_attendance` rows are actual named check-ins. One person/gathering pair is retained even on duplicate submissions. CRM `resolveCommitment` and `setAttendancePlan(attended)` use the same backend workflow as service/meeting recording. Expected, confirmed, away and pending responses alone never create attendance.
- Service totals consist of named counts plus explicit unnamed counts. Adding/removing a named check-in preserves the unnamed portion; it does not assume the person was included in an unnamed headcount. The service form lets an administrator correct the complete observed totals. Counts must be non-negative whole numbers, categories cannot exceed the total, and totals cannot be lower than named records. Meeting totals are present named people plus unnamed guests.
- The service and meeting forms save details/check-ins atomically. Their per-form request IDs survive a retry after an uncertain save. The care form also sends a request ID, and completing care against a source task is idempotent. New connected recording through these forms requires the live backend; demo mode explicitly rejects these writes instead of claiming disconnected attendance/care success. Legacy demo meeting helpers remain for illustrative fixtures, not the connected recording workflow.
- CRM actual attendance selects an existing compatible gathering. No matching date produces a useful error; multiple matches require explicit selection. The Sunday dialog shows time/location. Generic date commitments remain pending when multiple gatherings make automatic matching ambiguous; explicitly resolving that commitment links the selected gathering. Member attendance plans can reflect an existing Sunday check-in even if no plan was entered first.
- Actual attendance cannot be recorded for a future gathering. Backdated gatherings determine first-visit dates and first-timer/programme markers. Milestone provenance preserves earlier manually recorded history, later manual corrections and pre-existing salvation decisions; removing derived attendance restores the preserved values.
- Linked commitments, promise timeline dates, plans, person promise counts and open welcome-task dates reconcile on correction/removal. A date change that collides with another commitment/plan fails atomically instead of silently combining distinct expectations. Promise counts are rebuilt rather than incremented on retries. Welcome work is generated once per person and completed/cancelled welcome tasks are retained.
- Legacy service attendance create/update/bulk/sync/delete calls use the shared workflow. Legacy `follow_ups.resolvePromise` and `bulkResolvePromises` are explicitly retired; the error directs callers to service/meeting attendance or CRM commitments.

## Care, cleanup and recovery policies

- A linked care interaction owns one `follow_ups` row through `source_visitation_id`. Create/edit/removal keeps its date, method, status, notes and cached contact history coherent. Last-follow-up dates use chronological history rather than write order. Linked care history requires the confidential-care grant, including when read through follow-up APIs.
- Editing an open next action corrects its assignee/date/details. Removing the next-action requirement or cancelling care cancels open generated work. Completed/cancelled next-task links remain; note/date edits never reopen them or create a replacement. Additional work is an explicit new care task. Completed source tasks remain completed on correction or deletion. Reparenting terminal linked work is rejected.
- A legacy care row without a history backlink adopts a unique interaction with the same person, performer and creation timestamp, including old note/date drift. Ambiguous matches fail for manual review. Historical rows whose person/performer were previously changed may require an operator to establish the correct link; no bulk migration or guessed cleanup was run on existing data.
- Service/meeting deletion snapshots the gathering, check-ins, related plans/commitments/tasks and affected person state, then removes attendance, removes related plans and cancels commitments/open dated gathering tasks. Date-only expectations are cleaned up only when the deleted gathering is the unique match. Service photo metadata is included in the snapshot and removed from active relations; stored file bytes are retained for recovery.
- Care deletion snapshots the care record, linked history, generated tasks and source task; removes its timeline entry, cancels generated open work and clears its task backlinks. Completed tasks remain as completed work.
- `record_recovery` snapshots are administrator-only and have **no public restore API**. Recovery is an operator-reviewed procedure: inspect the snapshot, reconcile current references/conflicts, restore through authoritative mutations, and verify derived views. This is recovery evidence, not an automatic undo or a replacement for Task 06 backups. Deletion copy explains the policy.
- Follow-up task creation, completion, commitments, reactivation, legacy interaction logging and new care logging respect do-not-contact/pause rules. Time-based review may resume an expired pause, but never do-not-contact. Attendance itself remains recordable for paused/do-not-contact people without automatically scheduling contact or unpausing them. Corrections to historical care remain possible.

## Permission boundary

All public APIs retain `queryFor`/`mutationFor`. Actual attendance and its corrections require an administrator, as established by Task 02. Leaders can manage assigned expectations and follow-up; an attempt to change actual attendance or add a contradictory late commitment is rejected with a useful message. Actor attribution and recovery writes pass through the shared audit boundary. Automatic member attendance plans may have no historical leader; the authenticated acting account remains in the audit.
